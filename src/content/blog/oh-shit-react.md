React doesn't do shit. It is a failed promise of an older era, caught between quarterly reports and ever breaking version updates, and now shall be besmirched by the plethora of slop driven SEO everyone is after.

My biggest dissappointment is that a flawed framework is the one stochaistic parrots are best at. But being good at a shit framework usually also results in shitty code.

Why for lord's unknown reasons react chose to not solve any problem whatsoever regarding actual developer experience: data fetching and state management, reactive ui updates etc., and instead resorted to just abstracting away basic component abstraction via an API that half of the time behaves as if magical and the other time changes its working model slightly. You can't tell shit what a react code does unless you also specify a react version with it, upto the last decimal (semantic versioning means that the library is "belived" to be backwards compatible at the time of publishing).

Having burned away in refactoring messes between the various version "upgrades", half the community on the latest version, the other half still just finishing their previous refactor. Which version do you use? Then there is NextJS. It will be good one day, that is in its name, next. There is also another meaning in it, Next means Next to Shit. And shit in this case is react.

They are not looking 10 years in the future, they are instead looking 10 years back. Several problems with react have been solved, fortunately, not by react. But by other frameworks. Unfortunately, some of those innovative frameworks are shackelled by a lack of funding and .
