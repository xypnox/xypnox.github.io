---
title : "High Quality Audio"
date : 2025-12-20
---


From as far as I can remember I used to like music. My parents had a casette player and a lot of cassetes, most were either hymns and prayers, but a few were standard bollywood soundtracks and a rare few were albums. I remember gutting the inards of a few cassets in my grandparents home. I realized the sin I had commited much later in life.

The player was gone in a few years, abandoned or resold after we shifted due to my father's constantly shifting jobs. A few years later we got a sound system. It was on a clearance sale, a mall in a city about 60 kilometers away. A lot was purchased that day, for a sale was a significant driving factor in our financial situation. It was a home theater system from Sony and had 5 speakers with bass. It was the best thing I had heard (yet). I remember a song where the glass shattered and every shard was audible.

A few years later and another job switch, a shift of cities and my father got a HTC android phone from his company. During those days when htc was a direct competitor to Apple, it had partnered with Beats (before it was bought out by Apple). It was surreal, an in-ear _headdy_ experience that felt as if the music was just there in your head. Sublime.

At the start of my college I got an iem (Onemore piston) for whatever money I could set aside from the allowance. It was a nice experience. But they broke in a cycling accident, at least none of my bones broke, but definitely it was (almost) as painful. I got another pair, this time from KZ. They were similarly good and lasted me quite some time.

Once I had made some money from internships and after being influenced from the "Pop Tech" zeitgeist, I got a pair of AudioTechnica M50x. I remember watching a senior, playing music on them, he let me try them and I was quite impressed. The M50s did serve me well. They now lie in a dusty corner, a bit overused, with toothpicks and cardboard supporting the degrading plastic shell where it's own mechanisms had failed with time. I loved them. They were easy to wear, and had good tone, neutral playback, and decent bass. I used them for everything audio.

About 5 years ago, when the trend of true wireless earbuds popped up, I, smattered by the hype, got Sony's WXM something something. They were a marvel of engineering. Two pebble shaped pieces of plastic and silicon that could play audio without any wires. It was a piece of sci-fi magic, a leap forward. Or rather several leaps backwards. They cost me a lot of money, with negligible improvement in audio quality over my ten times cheaper IEMs. They lay unused for majority of the time, only ocassionally being played during flights (they had decent noise cancellation). Moreover, the memory foam tips would fall apart in front of your eyes. I still regret getting them.

Then for a few years, I did not spend anything for audio quality at all. It was all sufficient. I used the M50s and IEMs, I streamed music, watched videos, played games. Things sounded just fine.

Then the old dreams snuck in. When I got the iems, I had looked around, and at that time the best bang-for-buck audio equipment was the Sennheiser HD 600. A bit too expensive back then. It's allure had not left me yet. So once I was set on a headphone upgrade, (the M50 had sounded great till then and I had no other reference), I got the HD 600. Unfortunately in terms of monetary expenses it was not the end of my woes. The damned headphone is so good it can't be used with everyday gadget's 3.5mm jacks. (They don't have the power to drive the 300 ohm drivers at reasonable volumes.) So I had to get a desktop dac+amp. I snatched the Topping DX1.

The headphones arrived a day before the dac. And I drove them through my laptop's 3.5mm jack at 100% volume to get somewhat of a muffled low-volume audio. It was amazing. I had not listened to anything like that apart from attending a live instrument concert. I remember myself lying in uncomfortable positions (that I didn't realize were uncomfortable because the music had taken over my mind). And then the next day the dac arrived. _That was the day I understood what music actually was._ What the music producers and composers did. I could hear each instrument, separately. And it was all clear, even to earsplitting volumes. (which is why we should be careful with excellent audio gear, it negates our expectations for volume vs distortion.)

It sounds a thousand times better than the wxm earbuds. I realized I was searching for audio quality, convenience was secondary.

Technologically, it is quite ancient in this 'AI' world. It was designed and developed in the 90s. And apart from some minor housing changes it has remained the same through all these years of production. And Senny still makes them to this day. Without any changes to its drivers or sound signature. While every year a seemingly new technology makes the previous year's phones obsolete. (ahem.. ahem.. such as degraded battery life on future software updates).

If you have an eye prescription you know what the difference is between blurry and clear vision. The HD 600 screamed how blurry my ears had been to sound.

There are various sciences and technology that go into making audio reproduction possible at undiscernable levels of details, but good audio gear lets you understand and appreciate that these marvels of engineering are nothing without the soul of the artists singing into the machnines. You can hear when the singer inhales between the pauses (if they were old or brave enough to let it remain in the track unedited), you can hear the fingers sliding by the strings when the guitarist picks another chord. The instrumets play together, not on top of each other. The silence between the symphony is louder. If you play a live recording, you might as well be there.

In many forms of media, a prevailing concept invades all such discussions of communication: _Is it just how the artist intended it to be?_ Initially it seems like a stupid question, how can it not be? But then there are differences, like the color-balance of a monitor, there is the entire audio chain, from the way it was recorded, to how it was mixed and mastered, to how it was played, on what equipment with what EQ. How precise is the output when compared to what was in the input? _Is the artist's red your red?_

The HD 600s lie somewhere in the transparent category, they lack a little base, but the rest of the sound is very lifelike. Voices, drums, guitars, and various other acoustic instruments sound real. Are they just like what the artist intended? (Perhaps only the artist can verify). Regardless, a good communication channel reveals the media instead of embellishing it with neon lights. _You should hear good audio as good and bad audio as bad._ Which the HD 600 does to a fault. Some of the songs that I used to enjoy in my blurry era were laid barren in clarity, the poor recording, mastering and the lack of soul were harder to ignore.

These beauties forced me to also evaluate how I consumed my audio. I have to be honest I can't actually differentiate lossless from compressed 320kbps audio, but the idea of listening to the uncompressed audio was so alluring, I had to setup a home server with music streaming. Tales of that adventure will be recounted in a later post. Anyway, I now usually listen to music from my server and haven't looked back. (And no I am no Apple shill to go and use their lossless service (which is the only feasible lossless option in India)).

With such majesty from the sennys, I was left to question how far did the rabbit hole go? I have recently acquired a few new pieces of equipment but I have not had the time to properly appreciate them yet, hence their absence from this tale.

If you have not yet experienced high resolution audio equipment, I implore you to give them a try, Ask around! Someone might have something that you can try before being convinced to splurge huge amounts on diminishing returns. If you had been using consumer grade equipment, it doesn't mean you have been listening to trash all this time, you have been listening to masterpieces through trash equipment.
