# Culture

My effort in cataloging the books at [Library/Books](/library/books) remains in a stale state as of publishing this article as I am working on my own solutions, and will update this when I have finished setting up my reading history and booklists there. 

For now you are awarded with a more direct and personal overview of books I have read in the period of 2024.

My preferred genres have largely remained the same. I still enjoy _fantasy, fiction, and classics_ and have started sprinkling a few _detective mysteries_ in between.

Once ["The Three-body problem"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three-Body_Problem_(novel)) was in the zeitgeist (at that time), I had to watch it (where I was wrong). I stumbled across a few reviews from people that had both read and watched the trilogy and I knew I had to read it first (where I was right). The trilogy weaves a multi-dimensional story while explaning extraordinary events with our current understanding of physics. I have not watched the series yet (and now, I don't know if I ever will). Its the kind of fiction where your mind will build the fictional world far better than any live-action drama could provide.


[Watchmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchmen) dawned upon me after I had finished The Sandman last year. It is from a similar era of darker grittier comics. The story this time is set in a world of vigilanttes with superpowers. It is not clearly separated into right and wrong. The story focuses more on the world than the powers in specific and the crescendo at the end is phenomenal.

Between the larger pieces of text, I got time to read ["The Giving Tree"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Giving_Tree) by Shel Silverstein. It is a short read, perhaps hardly taking more than 10 mins. It contains no overarching lessons by the author, no moral obligations. It just depicts a few events in the life of a human and a tree. The beauty of the story is in the questions it allows us to frame. What would you do if you were the tree? What if you were the human?

In a search for some thrill, I gave [The Girl with the Dragon Tattoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Girl_with_the_Dragon_Tattoo) a go. It is a nice book but feels excessively long. The ending is somewhat decent but I have not had the motivation to continue the series. It has decent themes and discusses relevant problems. However, its plot is not as tight and clean as I like my fictional books to be.

I also purchased a dozen physical books from a book store, having only able to finish the smallest of the bunch. [The Slow Regard of Silent Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Slow_Regard_of_Silent_Things) is a sideline story about a character in the main trilogy of The kingkiller Chronicle released in 2014. Leaving the incomplete (as of now) trilogy aside, the book offers a fresh view on stories, and our expectations of stories, of protagonists, of characters, of goals, of mysteries and of endings. It is a book with a different kind of story.

Of the rest of the physical books, a few are on specific topics such as illusions and animations. The others are aimed as a collection of inspirations for graphic design.

In my teenage years, I often read fiction and fantasy, but occasionally I read and reread the single best detective fiction universe ever created. the one and the only, [The Complete Sherlock Holmes](https://sherlock-holm.es/). To quench the same thirst, I chose a few books from the ["Hercule Poirot"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercule_Poirot) Novels of Agatha Christie. They were enjoyable, each story carrying the expected (and the unexpected) twists and turns. It deploys the classic trope of berating a fictional detective novel inside a fictional detective novel (its as spicy as always). Unfortunately, in most cases, the detective is (magically) found in possession of very specific information that applies extremely cleanly to the current case with exceptional precision. Tropey or not, the events of Christie's novels still feel "just" (in a idealistic realistic sort of way) and the protagonist remains exceptionally charming.

## Games

I have continued playing games and have enjoyed them (to a certain extent). ## Gadgetry

The PC I built a few years ago has lasted well. I upgraded the storage with an additional terabyte of SSD and switched the case with [LANCOOL 207 - Lian Li](https://lian-li.com/product/lancool-207/) (ofcourse in white).  

 Firstly, I am starting to have a bad experience with a bunch of logitech mice. My first one, a venerable G201 built scroll click issues and the latest to fall, [G502X](https://www.logitechg.com/en-us/products/gaming-mice/g502-x-wired-lightforce.910-006144.html) started double clicking the side buttons. Moreover the wire started fraying badly 2 years in. The G502X cost me a decent amount of money, perhaps a little too much for a mice, and didn't last 2 years without developing problems.  

I replaced it with the ["Orochimaru"](https://www.razer.com/gaming-mice/razer-orochi-v2). The new mice is quite light, and small. Built well, it is a pleasure to use. And until recently I had sorely missed the extra mouse buttons on the G502X. I had bound them to the volume controls. Even though I have a rotary encoder on the keyboard, reaching for something in your hand is easier than something close at hand. So I spent a couple of months with the new mouse feeling a little sad. To my dismay I discovered the Razer's customization software. And they have done a great job at it. The preferences are saved to the mouse requiring no running apps (which also means it works flawlessly with linux). And the killer feature? The hypershift mode which I bound to the DPI button. (I don't switch the DPI anyway.) Now the side buttons in hypershift act as volume up and down and it works on device, no running software needed. I use a AAA battery in the AA slot with [the ingenious foil mod](https://www.reddit.com/r/MouseReview/comments/n3qted/the_best_weight_distribution_option_for_the_razer/).

I also got a laptop for mobile work, considering that the tower PC is mighty immobile. The laptop I chose was entry level [Thinkpad e14 gen 6](https://www.lenovo.com/in/en/p/laptops/thinkpad/thinkpade/lenovo-thinkpad-e14-gen-6-14-inch-amd/21m3s08500) with base specs and a ryzen 5. Apart from upgrading the case from plastic to metal and choosing a high def display, I let the rest be as they were specced and later upgraded the ram by buying a 16gb stick boosting it to 24gb ram, which is mighty enough for browsing, development and a few containers. 

It should be kept in mind that I bought it from my own dough, not sponsored by any corporation, thus my control over my laptop is more important for me than a few minor inconviniences like fixing a few kernel flags at initial setup to get the brightness and volume keys to work. The best feature is that maligned corporations cannot autoship slop stupidity (come on it ain't intelligent!) to my machines. Moreover running linux means that my hardware will not become obsolete on the whims of michaelsoft and their TPM module (better known as fake obsoletion technology in the name of security to force large corporations and normal users to upgrade their hardware which would work just fine with linux.)
